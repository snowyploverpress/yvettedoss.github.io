Yvette Doss

Ph.D. Candidate in Education (Special Education, Disability, and Risk Studies) at UC Santa Barbara.

I study how schools and educational technologies shape access and opportunity for autistic and neurodivergent students.

About

I am on the 2026–27 academic job market for tenure-track positions in special education and teacher education.

I work in special education teacher preparation and study inclusion and access for autistic and neurodivergent students across K–12 and higher education. My current work includes research on autistic college students’ use of AI tools and K–12 autistic student inclusive placement.

My research asks how institutions, technologies, and learning environments organize or limit meaningful access for neurodivergent students.

Before my doctoral work, I spent six years as a special education teacher in LAUSD across elementary and secondary settings. I serve as a university supervisor for Education Specialist candidates in their field placements, and I have served as a teaching assistant in the UCSB Teacher Education Program and across several education courses. I also founded the Neurodivergent Student Project, a peer-support initiative for neurodivergent students.

Teaching

My approach to teaching is shaped by six years in LAUSD classrooms and ongoing work in teacher preparation. I am committed to Universal Design for Learning as both a pedagogical framework and an equity stance, one that assumes learner variability as the norm rather than the exception. At the postsecondary level, I design learning experiences that help students connect abstract educational ideas to the real decisions teachers must make about access, inclusion, and learner difference.

Research

Across K–12 placement, teacher preparation, and AI tools, my research asks one question: how do educational environments organize access for autistic and neurodivergent students, and who ends up doing the work of access when they don’t? I study this at three levels: the district, the classroom, and the technologies students use.

Selected research
Classroom

Disability, Language, and Mathematics Learning

Collaborative qualitative research, supported by the Spencer Foundation, on how Latina middle school students with learning disabilities learn mathematics in a dual immersion school. A first-authored book chapter from this work is accepted.

Technology · Dissertation in progress

Who Does the Work? Cognitive Labor Redistribution and Autistic College Student Experience in Human-AI Interaction

My dissertation examines how cognitive labor shifts when autistic college students work with AI tools, developing a framework for evaluating how different divisions of labor support or undermine competence, autonomy, and authentic attunement. Its first study, on how autistic Redditors adapt ChatGPT, was presented at AERA 2026.

Teacher preparation

Deeper Learning in Teacher Education Programs

Graduate Student Researcher on a national qualitative study, funded by the Hewlett Foundation, examining deeper learning practices across teacher education programs through interviews with teacher candidates and faculty.

District

District Variation in Least Restrictive Environment Placement in California

A study of district-to-district variation in the educational placement of autistic students across California, using four years of state program-setting data. The study asks which district characteristics predict inclusive placement and treats inclusion as a matter of district capacity. A paper is under review for the 2027 AERA Annual Meeting.
